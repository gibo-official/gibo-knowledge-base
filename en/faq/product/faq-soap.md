### Q: Is laser the same as infrared? What is TOF? [🟢T3-Base] | sensor

A: Strictly speaking, except for ultraviolet light and visible light, all others belong to infrared light. Laser refers to photons generated by stimulated emission, a process where a certain light (such as infrared, red, blue, etc.) is strengthened or weakened through a laser generator and emitted. Currently what we apply can be considered infrared light.
ToF: Time of Flight. ToF is actually a distance measurement method. By measuring the "flight time" of signals such as ultrasound/microwave/light between the emitter and reflector, the distance between the two is calculated.
Data generated and captured using ToF sensors is very useful because it can provide depth information. Combined with cameras, 3D images can be obtained, enabling pedestrian detection, user identity verification based on facial features, environmental mapping using SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) algorithms, etc. ToF sensors are widely used in mobile phones, robots, smart vehicles, AR, and various other fields.

### Q: What's the difference between power-off protection and low-voltage reminder? [🟢T3-Base] | sensor

### Q: What is the sensor module output signal? [🟢T3-Base] | sensor

A: General output signals are pulse signals or level signals. Level signals are signals represented by level values, divided into high level "1" and low level "0". Pulse signals are emitted continuously at a certain voltage amplitude and certain time interval. The time interval between pulse signals is called the period; the number of pulses generated per unit time (such as 1 second) is called frequency.
Pulse solenoid valves are generally opened by positive pulse and closed by negative pulse.

### Q: Why does it still sense after my hand leaves the sensing range? What is tail distance? [🔴T1-SEO] | sensor

A: Tail distance can be understood as: when the hand enters the sensing range normally, the sensor starts working. During sensor operation, slowly moving in the opposite direction away from the sensor, when the explicitly stated sensing distance range is exceeded but the sensor is still working, continuing to move in the opposite direction until the sensor stops working. The distance measured from beyond the explicitly stated sensing distance to where sensing stops is considered tail distance.
Generally, sensors set a certain tail distance for better user experience, preventing frequent water on/off at the critical distance that affects user experience.
Generally, tail distance control does not exceed 20% of the explicitly stated sensing distance.
