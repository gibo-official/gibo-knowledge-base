## I. Product Introduction

The GBL-6193DB is GIBO's dual-sensor hand washer designed for demanding medical environments. The product uses an ABS chrome-plated shell, wall-mounted installation, with two independent sensing zones: the first sensing zone (upper, sensing distance 15cm±2cm) handles flow for hand washing, and the second sensing zone (lower/side, sensing distance 7cm±1cm) handles knee or body-part triggered shut-off. This dual-sensor design achieves true "fully zero-contact"—throughout the complete flow from starting the wash to ending the shut-off, the user's hands need not touch any device part, thoroughly eliminating secondary contamination risk.

Hospital operating rooms, ICU wards, sterile laboratories, pharmaceutical workshops—these scenarios with extremely high infection-control demands most fear the secondary contamination caused by "washing hands then using hands to shut off the water." The 6193DB is built for such demanding environments: the ABS material has a smooth surface that hardly harbors bacteria, and the chrome layer tolerates frequent wiping with the strong disinfectants commonly used in hospitals (such as alcohol and chlorine-based disinfectants) without corrosion or discoloration. The whole unit is powered by 3× AAA (size 7) batteries at DC4.5V, with no external power cord needed, simplifying the electrical installation complexity of medical spaces.

Combining the above: the 6193DB makes handwashing in high-risk venues truly "reach for flow, knee for stop, hands never touch the device," while being disinfectant-resistant and wireless. The three points below are the product values most worth highlighting.

## II. Features

### 2.1 Upper/Lower Zoned Dual-sensor

- **First sensing zone (upper)**: Located on the upper part of the hand washer, sensing distance 15cm±2cm; reach or arm near triggers flow. Suited to normal hand-wash motions—naturally reaching under the outlet starts it.
- **Second sensing zone (lower)**: Located on the lower side of the hand washer, shorter sensing distance (7cm±1cm), requires active nearing to trigger. Designed for knee push or body nearing to trigger shut-off. The two zones are physically separated to avoid false triggering.

### 2.2 Wall-mounted Compact Design

The round baseplate has four mounting screw holes (M4 spec) at its corners for stable, reliable wall mounting. The overall compact shape is unobtrusive, suited to space-limited positions such as beside an operating-room scrub sink, by an ICU bed, or on the side of a lab bench. A chrome decorative ring with the white ABS body forms a simple, professional look.

### 2.3 Standardized Outlet Interface

The bottom is equipped with a standard threaded outlet that can connect to various aerators or straight-spray spouts. The chrome aerator gives even, gentle flow, suited to long hand washing without splashing.

### 2.4 Battery Power + Low Consumption

DC4.5V power (3× AAA alkaline batteries in series). The ultra-low-power circuit design gives a relatively long battery life (specific duration to be verified, depending on frequency). The battery compartment is an independent sealed box, making battery replacement simple without affecting waterproof performance.

### 2.5 Timeout Safety Protection

Has an auto shut-off on flow timeout (specific timeout to be verified), preventing continuous long flow caused by failing to trigger the second sensing zone. This mechanism is both an energy-saving measure and a safety guarantee.

### 2.6 Quick Drain Design

A drain guide structure is set below the outlet, directing the water after hand washing smoothly into the sink / collection container below, avoiding flow along the device exterior that would stain the wall.

## III. Core Selling Points

### Selling Point 1: Medical-grade Zero-contact — Dual-sensor Eliminates Cross-infection

The first sensing zone flows on reach; the second sensing zone uses the knee or any body part to trigger shut-off. Throughout the whole hand-wash flow, the hands touch no device part at all, blocking the contact-transmission path at the root. For high-risk scenarios such as operating-room scrubbing and ICU bedside hand washing, this is an indispensable infection-control measure.

### Selling Point 2: ABS Chrome Disinfectant-resistant — Fits Harsh Medical Cleaning Rules

The ABS chrome-plated shell tolerates daily multiple wipes with hospital-common strong disinfectants such as alcohol, chlorine disinfectant, and peroxide—no corrosion, no discoloration, no cracking. The smooth, dense surface hardly harbors bacteria, meeting the high-frequency, high-standard environmental cleaning requirements of medical institutions.

### Selling Point 3: Pure Battery Wireless Install — No Reserved Power, Flexible Deployment

Powered by 3× AAA batteries (DC4.5V), a fully wireless design. No need to embed wires in the wall or install power sockets, greatly lowering the retrofit cost and construction complexity of medical venues. Battery replacement is simple; maintenance staff can complete it in minutes.

## V. Installation Instructions

### 5.1 Before Installation

- Select the wall mounting height (generally outlet 100~120cm from the floor, or adjust by user height)
- Ensure the position is within reach of the hand and that the knee/thigh can reach the second sensing zone

### 5.2 Precautions

- Do not spray disinfectant directly into the sensor window or battery compartment
- Ensure hands are dry when replacing batteries
- When unused for a long time, close the inlet angle valve and remove the batteries

### 5.3 Installation Steps

1. Drill holes in the wall per the baseplate's four corners (generally use M4 or M5 expansion screws); mount the baseplate flat against the wall and tighten the four fixing screws
2. Connect the inlet hose (user-supplied, generally PE or PVC) to the back inlet interface of the hand washer; the other end to the angle valve or water source (use a suitable washer to prevent leakage)
3. Open the battery compartment cover on the back or side; insert 3× AAA alkaline batteries (observe polarity)
4. Open the water source angle valve; first test whether the upper sensing flow is normal, then test whether the lower sensing stop function is sensitive; adjust mounting height or angle if optimization is needed

### 5.4 Power-on Self-check

After opening the water source angle valve, first test whether the upper sensing flow is normal, then test whether the lower sensing stop function is sensitive; adjust mounting height or angle if needed to optimize triggering.

### 5.5 Maintenance & Care (Medical Environment)

- **Daily**: Wipe the shell and sensor window with the disinfectant specified by the hospital (avoid spraying electronic components directly)
- **Weekly**: Check battery level (observe whether flow strength weakens; replace if weakened)
- **Monthly**: Check the inlet hose connection for leaks; check whether the fixing screws are loose
- **Quarterly**: Comprehensive check of sensing sensitivity; clean the inside of the sensor window if necessary (by professional staff)

## VI. Application Scenarios

### 6.1 Hospital Operating Room

Essential equipment for surgeons' pre-operative scrubbing. The dual-sensor design ensures a sterile scrub—reach for flow to start scrubbing, use elbow/body to touch for stop, hands remain sterile throughout.

### 6.2 ICU / Intensive Care Ward

Bedside hand washer. Medical staff quickly wash hands before/after patient care; knee stop avoids contact contamination.

### 6.3 Sterile Laboratory / Biosafety Cabinet Side

Hand-washing facility inside P2/P3 level laboratories. Zero-contact operation prevents cross-contamination in the hand-cleaning step before/after entering the core operation zone.

### 6.4 Pharmaceutical Clean Workshop

Hand-hygiene station for employees on the pharmaceutical production line. Meets the strict requirements of GMP for sanitation facilities; ABS material tolerates the cleaners commonly used in the pharmaceutical industry.

### 6.5 Food Processing Plant / Central Kitchen

Hand-wash station in food-safety-sensitive areas. Prevents staff hands from secondarily contacting equipment and contaminating food.

### 6.6 Pet Hospital / Veterinary Clinic

Hand-wash isolation measures when handling infectious cases, reducing the risk of zoonotic pathogen spread.
