## I. Product Introduction

The **Dual-Channel Remote Pneumatic Flush Fitting BC-KZH-YH01** is a wireless pneumatic flush assembly installed inside the toilet cistern, comprising two parts: a wireless sensor box and a wireless pneumatic control box. The sensor box is attached where a wave of the hand is convenient; the control box connects to the air pump and air valve and is hidden inside the cistern. It solves the most awkward problems of toilet flushing—old-style fittings require bending to press, and public press panels get dirty; single-path flushing either fails to clean or wastes too much water; and when the cistern is in the wall and the control box is far away, wiring is hard and so is repair.

Elderly-friendly home bathrooms, public restrooms, hotel rooms, and accessible toilets all need either non-contact operation, tiered water saving, or installation-position flexibility. The KZH-YH01 is built for exactly this "don't want to press, want tiers, and hard to wire" environment.

It uses 2.4G RF for wireless communication, reaching ≥8 meters in open space and controllable even through walls; a wave triggers the half flush and a long sense triggers the full flush, with two air-valve paths opening/closing separately—half flush saves water, full flush cleans thoroughly. The sensor box uses 2 AAA batteries with standby current ≤40μA; the control box uses a DC6V/1A adapter with standby ≤200μA—long-term standby without draining power. Flush time self-adapts to voltage: at 6.0V half/full flush is about 800ms; when voltage drops to 4.0V it auto-extends to 1810/2150ms, so low voltage still flushes clean. Air pump DC4.5V/650mA, air valve DC4.5V/150mA; sensor box life >500,000 cycles, pump/valve >200,000 cycles.

Put together, the KZH-YH01 lets the toilet "flush at a wave, separate half/full flush, wireless control through walls, and low maintenance once installed." The three points below are the product values we believe are most worth highlighting.

## II. Features

### 2.1 Dual-Path Pneumatic, Separate Half/Full Flush

The control box has a built-in air pump and two air-valve paths (Valve A for half flush, Valve B for full flush). A wave triggers the half flush and a long sense (1~7s) triggers the full flush, with the two paths opening/closing separately—half flush saves water, full flush cleans thoroughly—one fitting covers different flush volumes for urination and defecation.

### 2.2 2.4G Wireless Remote, Controllable Through Walls

The wireless sensor box and pneumatic control box communicate via 2.4G RF, ≥8 meters in open space and penetrating reinforced-concrete walls. The sensor box attaches on the convenient exterior side; the control box (air pump/valve) hides in the cistern—no wiring needed between cistern and wall, especially convenient for retrofitting old bathrooms.

### 2.3 Wave Sensing, Non-Contact Flushing

The sensor box receives a wave (≤1s) signal to trigger the half flush and a long sense (1~7s) to trigger the full flush, with no physical contact throughout. Reduces contact and cross-infection in public spaces and elderly-care scenarios, and avoids dirty press panels and mechanical wear.

### 2.4 Graded Low-Voltage Alarm, Buzzer Plus Flashing Light

Sensor box voltage <2.7V: green light slow-flashes 5 times but still operates; <2.2V: fast-flashes 10 times and stops operating. Control box <4.8V: buzzer sounds 5 times but still works; <4.5V: long buzz 1.5s five times and closes valve. Graded early warning lets maintenance replace batteries or check power in time, avoiding sudden failure.

### 2.5 One-Touch Pairing, 30-Second Match

A 30-second pairing window opens within 30 seconds before power-on; following the procedure, continuous sensing triggers the red LED to blink and enter pairing; within 30s the control box is powered on to complete binding. After pairing, the sensor box controls only the paired control box, not interfering with other devices—even when multiple stalls are installed side by side.

### 2.6 Low Standby, Durable Batteries

Sensor box: 2 AAA batteries, standby ≤40μA@3.0V; control box: adapter-powered, standby ≤200μA@6.0V. The low-power design means the wireless end does not need frequent battery changes, and also reduces adapter no-load loss.

### 2.7 Anti-Interference, Anti-Static, Stable in Engineering

ESD level 4 (air discharge ±15KV, contact discharge ±8KV), fast transient burst ±4KV normal operation; 3 same-model units installed at 50cm spacing actuate separately or simultaneously with no false operation. Complies with MIIT micro-power short-range radio requirements, so batch engineering deployment stays within compliance.

## III. Core Selling Points

### Selling Point 1: Separate Half/Full Dual-Path—Water-Saving and Clean

One fitting, two flush volumes: wave for half flush on urination, long sense for full flush on defecation, water on demand. A public toilet or hotel stall saves considerable flush water per year; beyond water bills, cistern refill and network load also drop. Half and full flush take separate air-valve paths,operate independently—no "want to save but can't clean" compromise.

### Selling Point 2: 2.4G Wireless Remote—Installable and Controllable Through Walls

Sensor box on the wall exterior, control box hidden in the cistern, 2.4G through-wall communication ≥8 meters. Old-bathroom retrofits need no wall-breaking wiring; aging-in-place retrofits attach the sensor box beside the grab bar or at wheelchair height so the elderly can flush without turning or bending. This point sells well for both home retrofits and public-toilet upgrades—low construction cost, fast handover.

### Selling Point 3: Low-Voltage Alarm Plus Anti-Interference—Fewer Engineering Callbacks

Both sensor box and control box have graded low-voltage alarms—light flash and buzzer graded prompts tell you when to change batteries or check power, so it won't suddenly stop flushing mid-use. Add ESD level 4, burst ±4KV, and no cross-talk at 50cm spacing for multiple units, and engineering batch deployment shows markedly fewer service tickets. For a property manager overseeing dozens of stalls, one less trip is real savings.

## V. Installation Instructions

### 5.1 Before Installation

1. Confirm control box power is DC6V/1A adapter; prepare 2 AAA batteries for the sensor box.
2. Confirm working water pressure 0.05~0.8MPa, usable water temperature 4~75°C.
3. Plan the sensor box mounting position (convenient, not long-term obstructed); fix the control box inside the cistern.
4. For side-by-side multiple stalls, reserve ≥50cm spacing to avoid mutual interference during installation.

### 5.2 Notes

- Disconnect control box power and remove sensor box batteries before assembly/disassembly.
- The control box must be reliably grounded, on a separate adapter circuit.
- Do not hot-plug sensor box terminals to avoid arcing.
- The 30 seconds after power-on is the pairing window; do not accidentally trigger pairing outside the window.

### 5.3 Installation Steps

1. Fix the wireless pneumatic control box inside the cistern; connect the air pump, air valve, and adapter.
2. Mount the wireless sensor box at the chosen position; insert 2 AAA batteries.
3. Disconnect control box power; follow the pairing procedure to put the sensor box into pairing (red LED blinks).
4. Within 30s, power on the control box; buzzer long buzz 1.5s one tone indicates successful pairing.
5. Simulate a wave (half flush) and long sense (full flush) respectively to verify both paths.
6. Pair multiple devices one by one; confirm no cross-control.
7. Turn on water and test flush; confirm half/full flush volume and return are normal.

### 5.4 Power-On Self-Check and Pairing

On power-on, the control box buzzer gives a short "beep" indicating startup; after successful pairing the buzzer gives a long "beeep" of 1.5s one tone. When the sensor box receives a signal, the green LED ring flashes 1 time (half flush) or 2 times (full flush) as status indication.

### 5.5 Battery Replacement & Maintenance

When sensor box voltage drops below 2.7V and the green light slow-flashes 5 times, replace batteries; below 2.2V it stops and must be replaced. Replacement: take out sensor box → replace 2 same-model AAA batteries → power on again. For control box low voltage, follow the buzzer graded prompt to check the adapter power.
