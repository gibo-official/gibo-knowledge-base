## 1. Product Introduction

The **Lan'e L-31516 Sensor Flush Valve Cartridge** is an infrared sensor flush-control assembly installed inside the cistern of a toilet or squat pan. It consists of a sensor head, a drive mechanism, and a battery box, and is responsible for controlling the cistern fitting to complete the flush. It addresses the most practical pain points in public and residential restrooms: mechanically pressed fittings require bending down to push, get dirty and break easily; single-mode sensors either fail to flush clean or waste too much water; and public-restroom installations worry about false triggers from strong light, ESD damage to the board, and water ingress.

Schools, factories, stations, township public restrooms, and hotel rooms—these places use a large number of cistern fittings and require frequent maintenance, and most fear the situation of "should flush but doesn't, shouldn't flush but flushes randomly, broken and nobody knows." The L-31516 is built for such high-frequency, public, and rugged-duty scenarios.

It uses infrared sensing: a quick wave triggers the large flush, while a wave with a 1-second pause triggers the small flush, separating flush volumes for urination and defecation. Operating voltage is 4.5–6V, powered by 4 AA alkaline batteries, with standby power consumption <40μA, so it draws virtually no power during long standby. Its anti-interference is solid: with a 1500-lumen interfering light source obliquely shining from 1.5 m, sensing distance reduction is less than 5%, with no false triggering and no lock-up. It passes ESD level 4 (air discharge ±15KV, contact discharge ±8KV), national standard level 4 for fast transient burst (EFT), and electromagnetic radiation level 2 (3V/m). The entire module submerged in 25℃ water for 24 hours shows no water ingress and functions normally, unfazed by the high-humidity environment of public restrooms. Low-voltage indication is in two stages: when voltage drops below 4.8V±0.1 it flashes 5 times but can still flush; below 4.5V±0.1 it fast-flashes 10 times and stops flushing, so maintenance can replace batteries in time.

Putting it all together: the L-31516 lets the cistern "flush with a wave, separate large and small flushes, fear neither strong light nor static, and need little maintenance once installed." The following three points are the product values we believe are most worth highlighting.

## 2. Features

### 2.1 Infrared Dual Flush — Separate Large and Small Flushes

The L-31516 features dual-flush sensor flushing. Compared with traditional mechanical press fittings, a wave is more convenient and non-contact is more hygienic. Urination and defecation correspond to different flush volumes, flushing cleanly without wasting water.

### 2.2 Quick Wave for Large Flush, Short Pause for Small Flush

A quick wave executes the large flush: the motor travels down to the large-flush stroke, pauses 1 second, then returns. A wave held for 1 second or more executes the small flush: it travels down to the small-flush stroke and likewise pauses 1 second before returning. After a trigger, sensing is suspended for 5 seconds, then resumes normally, avoiding continuous false flushing.

### 2.3 Low Standby Power Consumption, Durable on 4 Batteries

Operating voltage 4.5–6V, powered by 4 AA dry-cell batteries, standby power consumption <40μA (≤60μA at 6.0V). Low power lets the cistern fitting stay on standby without draining, so property management need not open the cover to replace batteries frequently.

### 2.4 Staged Low-voltage Indication, Advance Battery Warning

When voltage is below 4.8V±0.1, the sensor LED flashes 5 times but can still flush; when below 4.5V±0.1, the LED fast-flashes 10 times and stops flushing. The staged warning lets maintenance schedule battery replacement in time, avoiding sudden failure during use.

### 2.5 Strong-light and Interference Immunity, No False Triggers in Public Restrooms

With a 1500-lumen interfering light source obliquely shining from 1.5 m, the impact on sensing distance is small (reduction <5%)—no false triggering, no lock-up, no overcurrent. It operates stably under common light sources such as incandescent, fluorescent, halogen, daylight, bathroom heaters, and hair dryers sharing the same socket.

### 2.6 ESD Level 4 Plus Waterproofing — Rugged for Engineering Use

ESD level 4 (air discharge ±15KV, contact discharge ±8KV), national standard level 4 fast transient burst (EFT), and electromagnetic radiation level 2 (80M–1000MHz, 3V/m) all operate normally. After environmental testing, the module is immersed in 25℃ water for 24 hours with no water ingress and normal function, suitable for high-humidity public restrooms.

### 2.7 Reverse-polarity Protection, No Board Burnout on Miswiring

Reverse power connection will not cause board burnout; even if polarity is reversed during installation or maintenance, the control board is not damaged, reducing error costs in construction and after-sales service.

## 5. Installation Instructions

### 5.1 Before Installation

1. Confirm power is 4 AA dry-cell batteries of the same batch, no mixing old and new
2. Confirm operating water pressure 0.05~1.0MPa and applicable water temperature 4~55℃
3. Install the sensor head away from prolonged direct strong light and continuous obstruction
4. Before full assembly, power on the unit alone to verify sensing and flushing actions

### 5.2 Precautions

- Disconnect power (remove batteries) before installation/disassembly
- Reverse polarity won't burn the board, but wiring per markings is still recommended
- Do not hot-plug the sensor head terminals
- In high-humidity locations, ensure the module sealing surface is intact and do not damage the potting

### 5.3 Installation Steps

1. Install the drive mechanism into the corresponding position of the cistern fitting, and connect the sensor head and battery box
2. Fix the sensor head on the cistern lid or designated sensing surface, aligned with the user's movement path
3. Insert 4 AA dry-cell batteries of the same batch and close the battery box
4. Power on; LED lit for 2 seconds indicates startup
5. Verify large flush with a quick wave and small flush with a 1-second pause
6. Simulate low voltage (or observe a low-battery sample) to confirm staged indication is normal
7. When installing multiple stalls side by side, verify each unit individually for no mutual interference

### 5.4 Power-on Self-check

After power-on, the LED lights for 2 seconds then enters standby; a wave trigger makes the motor execute the corresponding stroke. No sensing occurs during the 5-second delay after a trigger, then it auto-resumes—this is normal anti-jitter logic.

### 5.5 Battery Replacement and Maintenance

When voltage is below 4.8V±0.1 and the LED flashes 5 times, schedule battery replacement; below 4.5V±0.1 it stops flushing and replacement is mandatory. Replacement: remove the battery box → replace with 4 new dry-cell batteries of the same brand → reinstall and power on. Bulk unified replacement is recommended.

## 6. Applicable Finished Products and Integration Solutions

### 6.1 Integration with Toilet / Squat Pan Cistern Assemblies

As a sensor control component, the L-31516 is embedded into various toilet and squat pan cisterns to form a non-contact dual-flush solution, suitable for high-frequency public places such as schools, factories, stations, and hotels.

The L-31516's sensor control can connect to a concealed flush-valve pre-embedded assembly (e.g., BC-YMH-8308 squat pan embedding box), triggering automatic flushing via the sensor signal—upgrading to a non-contact public-restroom solution without modifying the ceramic body.
