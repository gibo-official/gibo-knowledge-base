## I. Product Introduction

The **BC-YMH-8308 Concealed Squat Pan Pre-embedded Box** is a concealed squat-pan flush-valve assembly installed inside the bathroom wall. It is buried in the wall before tile is laid, leaving only a control panel exposed on the wall surface, while the flush-valve body, solenoid valve, and supply/drain piping are all hidden inside the wall. It solves the most troublesome problems of concealed squat pans in public restrooms: once a valve installed in the wall leaks or bursts, tearing up tiles for repair is extremely costly; in unheated northern toilets, frozen and cracked pipes are a common occurrence in winter; and with old-style flush valves, the moment the valve opens the "clang" of water hammer reverberates through the building's piping, gradually loosening joints and causing seepage over time.

Schools, factories, stations, scenic areas, and township public toilets use large numbers of concealed squat pans and dread after-sales rework the most—when something buried in the wall fails, the labor cost is ten times the price of the part. The 8308 is built for exactly this "install it and never touch it again" scenario.

It is driven by a single low-voltage solenoid valve rated 3.6~6.5V, with a coil resistance of 14±0.5Ω and a 10ms pulse trigger, consuming almost no standby power. The valve body features a low-water-hammer soft-closing design that reduces water surge to below 0.2MPa, so opening the valve does not shake the piping. More importantly, it is built tough: it holds 3.5MPa burst pressure for 60 seconds with no deformation or leakage; it operates normally after being frozen at -20°C for 24 hours under 0.3MPa upstream pressure; and its anti-siphon value of ≤20mm prevents backflow of sewage into the network. After 500,000 cycle life testing, the seal still meets specification, so a single unit lasts for many years.

Put together, the 8308 makes the concealed squat-pan flush valve "stable once embedded in the wall—no burst, no freeze, no hammer, no backflow—fewer callbacks after project handover." The three points below are the product values we believe are most worth highlighting.

## II. Features

### 2.1 Wall Pre-embedded, Only Panel Exposed After Finishing

The 8308 is installed in the wall as a pre-embedded box before tiling. The flush-valve body, solenoid valve, and supply/drain interfaces are all hidden inside the wall; only the control panel is exposed on the finished wall surface. In large-area installations such as public toilets and schools, the overall appearance is clean, and it also prevents exposed valves from being struck or maliciously damaged.

### 2.2 Low Water Hammer, Soft Closing—No Pipe Shaking or Noise

The internal water path and open/close logic are optimized to keep water surge at ≤0.2MPa. Compared with the "clang" of old-style flush valves on opening, the 8308 opens and closes smoothly—no pipe shaking, no loosened joints—especially beneficial for high-rise and long-pipeline projects.

### 2.3 Anti-Siphon Design, No Sewage Backflow

Tested per GB/T 26750-2011, the siphon value is ≤20mm, effectively preventing sewage from flowing back through the flush valve into the water supply system when the network is under negative pressure. This meets the backflow-prevention requirements for sanitary ware, ensuring smooth inspection and acceptance.

### 2.4 Freeze-Crack Resistant, Usable in the North

Under 0.3MPa upstream pressure, frozen at -20°C for 24 hours then thawed, both high- and low-pressure sealing performance remain normal and operation is unaffected. Unheated township public toilets and northern winter conditions will not crack at the first frost, reducing concentrated winter service calls.

### 2.5 No Burst Under Overpressure, Tough Valve Body

Strength performance: at 0.9±0.02MPa the valve body and all joints show no deformation or leakage; under extreme conditions the 3.5MPa burst pressure held for 60s still shows no deformation or leakage. Sudden overpressure from water-hammer spikes or pump-station pressure boosts is something the valve body can withstand.

### 2.6 Low-Voltage Solenoid Drive, Minimal Standby Power

Uses a 3.6~6.5V low-voltage solenoid valve with coil resistance 14±0.5Ω (20°C), 10ms pulse control, compatible with either battery or adapter. The low-power characteristic lets it pair with various sensor control boxes for long-term standby without draining power.

### 2.7 500,000 Cycles, Long-Term Maintenance-Free

After the fecal-flush valve completes 500,000 cycle life testing, sealing performance still meets requirements, with water output change before/after testing controlled within ±25%. For wall-embedded devices, longer life means a lower probability of tile-tearing repairs.

### Selling Point 1: Concealed Pre-embedding for a More Integrated Public-Toilet Finish

Exposed flush valves are easily bumped or vandalized, and look unsightly. The 8308 is fully buried in the wall, leaving only a panel on the wall surface—public spaces look tidier and the equipment is less likely to be tampered with. With water surge held below 0.2MPa, opening no longer "clangs" and shakes the piping; long water-transport pipelines are less prone to loosened, leaking joints. With anti-siphon value ≤20mm, the risk of sewage flowing back into the supply system is essentially eliminated. Both items pass GB/T 26750 and CJ/T 194-2014, so acceptance documentation is complete—no back-and-forth for rework.

### Selling Point 3: Freeze-Crack and Burst Resistant—Fewer Winter Callbacks Means Real Savings

Wall-embedded equipment fears freeze-cracking and burst pipes most—once a problem occurs, labor far exceeds the part itself. The 8308 works normally after freezing at -20°C for 24h and thawing, and withstands 3.5MPa overpressure for 60s without burst or leak, blocking the two most common categories of wall-rework hazards up front. For a 30-stall public-toilet project, avoiding even one tile-tearing repair saves more than half a day of labor plus materials; across many projects this adds up to real money.

## V. Installation Instructions

### 5.1 Before Installation

1. First flush the pipeline with open water to wash out sand, gravel, and rust, avoiding clogging the valve port.
2. Confirm water pressure is 0.05~0.8MPa; below 0.05MPa requires a booster.
3. An inlet strainer is recommended to prevent impurities entering the valve body.
4. The pre-embedded box should be slotted, placed, and piped before tile is laid.

### 5.2 Notes

- Always shut off water and disconnect power before installation or maintenance.
- In northern or winter shutdown scenarios, drain or insulate the piping to prevent freeze-cracking.
- When converting the union to 1-inch external thread, mind the direction and tightness of PTFE tape wrapping to avoid seepage.
- Pressure-test the in-wall piping and pass before closing the panel and laying tile.

### 5.3 Installation Steps

1. Slot the wall per drawing, place the pre-embedded box and initially secure it.
2. Connect the inlet (M39×1.5 to 1-inch external thread) and outlet (32mm tubing clamp) pipelines.
3. Level and reinforce to ensure the box is not skewed or loose.
4. Pressurize with water; confirm 0.9MPa strength and 0.6MPa sealing with no leakage.
5. Lay tile; after drying, install the control panel.
6. Connect the sensor control box or solenoid drive; power on for self-check.
7. Simulate a flush; confirm open/close and sealing are normal.

### 5.4 Power-On and Water Test

After water is on, apply leak-detection at joints or observe for 5 minutes to confirm no seepage; power on to trigger the solenoid valve, listen for the open/close sound and observe water output and return. If leakage is found during the water test, immediately close the valve and repair—never close the panel under pressure.

### 5.5 Maintenance & Storage

- Drain the valve body and piping before long-term shutdown or overwintering.
- Store in a ventilated, dry indoor area, not mixed with corrosive items; ambient temperature -10°C~55°C, relative humidity ≤95%RH.
- Prioritize access from the panel side for service to avoid damaging the embedded structure.
