## I. Product Introduction

The Single-cold Sensor Faucet is a sensor sanitary ware that delivers only cold water, uses infrared or dTOF Laser non-contact sensing, and is installed for high-frequency use on public basins—water flows when you reach in and auto-closes when you pull away, never touching the faucet switch. It solves the two most common problems at public restroom basins: first, contact transmission—dozens or hundreds of people in rotation twisting the same faucet handle, turning the handle into a node of cross-infection; second, false triggering and continuous flow—failure under strong window-side light, poor sensitivity in dim light, and equipment that constantly needs maintenance. Shopping malls, office buildings, airports and stations, star-rated hotels, hospitals, and school wash stations see heavy daily foot traffic with high hygiene and reliability demands, and most fear "no water when it should flow, wild spraying when it shouldn't." This product category typically goes the battery-powered wiring-free route—old-building renovations and points without reserved power can work by installing 4 AA batteries, no wall-breaking for power. GIBO's product line started in 2004 with MCU microcontroller sensor control; base models use 940nm active infrared reflection detection with Low-power Multi-stable Agile algorithm, while high-end models use 3rd-generation dTOF Laser ranging that directly measures photon round-trip time at millimeter-level accuracy, material-independent and unfazed by strong light; whole-unit standby power is suppressed to the 18μA level (about ≤0.2mW), with 4 batteries lasting 12 to 18 months. The valve body features low water hammer and self-cleaning anti-clogging treatment, resisting hard-water scaling and pipeline sediment. In summary: the Single-cold Sensor Faucet makes the public basin "deliver water when you reach in, no touching, no wild water flow by the window, and no worry for a year after installation." The three product values below are what we consider most worth highlighting.

## II. Features

### 2.1 Non-contact Sensing, Water on When You Reach In

The Single-cold Sensor Faucet detects hand proximity via infrared or dTOF Laser, automatically opening the valve to deliver water and delaying close on leaving, never touching the faucet throughout. A closed loop of four units—infrared detection, signal processing, MCU master control, and drive protection—collaborates, automatically calibrating the environmental baseline after power-on and dynamically adjusting the sensing threshold.

| Configuration Item | Description |
|--------|------|
| Sensing Form | 940nm active infrared reflection / dTOF Laser Time-of-Flight (by model) |
| Water-out Logic | Instant on when sensed, off when hand leaves; or long flow (open on reach, close on next reach) |
| Response Time | Water-out ≤0.6s, close ≤1s |
| Applicable Water Temperature | Cold water (single-cold type, inlet temperature per site supply) |

### 2.2 Dual-mode Water-out, One Unit Replaces Two

Public places and homes have different requirements for water-out logic: malls want "open on reach, close on next reach" rhythm, while home offices want "open on reach, close on leave" with zero wait. A single-cold sensor faucet can switch: long-flow mode auto-cuts after 180 seconds continuous (prevents continuous flow), instant-sensing mode closes on leaving with 60-second auto-cut. Installers need not stock two models for different floors, simplifying inventory and selection.

### 2.3 Battery Power, Wiring-free, Install-and-use for Old Buildings

Whole-unit static power is as low as 18μA level, working long-term with DC 6V (4×AA alkaline batteries, customer-supplied), no wall-breaking for power. Old-building renovations, points without power, and temporary locations install directly, reducing single-point installation from hours to dozens of minutes. Some models are dual AC/DC, running on either AC adapter or battery.

### 2.4 Low-voltage + Anti-continuous-flow Dual Protection

When voltage drops to about 4.6V, the LED flashes quickly to remind and cuts off water-out, prompting facility staff to replace batteries in time; continuous obstruction or over 60 seconds of continuous flow (180s for long flow) auto-cuts off, blocking "pot placed by window, person left forgetting to turn off" continuous flow. Low-battery reminder is clear, so it won't suddenly lose water mid-use.

### 2.5 Strong-light Immunity, No False Trigger by Window

Incandescent, fluorescent, LED, and halogen lamps at 45° oblique one meter away cause sensing distance change less than ±10%; multiple units powered simultaneously won't false-trigger each other. Basin by bay windows fears two things—false triggering with continuous flow under strong light, or complete failure under dim light—both covered by the dual-mode strong-light immunity algorithm.

### 2.6 Military-grade EMC, Stable in Complex Environments

Passes full EMC tests including ESD level 4 (±15KV), EFT burst level 4, and radiated immunity. Works normally beside mall escalators, variable-frequency air conditioners, large LED screens, and hospital CT rooms, with no mutual false action near daily appliances and rows of multiple units.

### 2.7 Low Water Hammer + Self-cleaning Anti-clogging, Rugged Against Water Quality

At close, water flow impact is gentle (peak differential ≤0.2MPa), fittings less likely to loosen; the valve core self-cleans at each open/close, with special fluid-dynamic shaping letting flow scour the valve core surface, resisting scaling in hard water and clogging from pipeline sediment and rust. In northern hard-water and old-pipeline scenarios, failure rates are significantly lower than conventional solutions.

## III. Core Selling Points

### Selling Point 1: Non-contact Water-out, Blocking Cross-infection Outside the Faucet

The most realistic problem at public restroom basins is dozens or hundreds of people in rotation twisting the same faucet handle, turning the handle into a relay baton for bacteria and viruses. The single-cold sensor faucet delivers water on reach and closes on leaving, zero contact throughout, cutting this transmission chain at the root. For hygiene-sensitive places like hospitals, schools, and transportation hubs, non-contact water use is not a plus but a hard requirement; the most direct benefit for facility staff is fewer "sticky faucet handle" hygiene complaints and cleaning burden.

### Selling Point 2: Four Batteries Last a Year, Renovation Without Pulling Wires

Many old-building wash stations want to switch to sensor faucets but get stuck on no reserved power. The single-cold sensor faucet has standby power just over 0.2mW, with 4 AA batteries lasting 12 to 18 months, install-and-use. A mall with 50 basins, if devices needed monthly battery replacement, would require 20-plus labor hours a year just climbing ladders; replacing once a year essentially zeroes that out. For old-building renovation, single-point cost drops from thousands of yuan for wiring to dozens of minutes of labor—real savings.

### Selling Point 3: No False Trigger Under Window Strong Light, Fewer Complaints and Trips for Facility Staff

Basins by bay windows—traditional infrared faucets basically "strike" under afternoon sunlight, users wave hands with no response and finally manually twist or simply don't wash; or conversely, self-start continuous flow under sunlight wasting water. The 3rd-generation dTOF Laser model doesn't judge by reflected light intensity but by timing, performing identically in west-facing windows, dim underground garages, and winter thick coats; infrared models also feature the dual-mode strong-light immunity algorithm, with sensing distance change less than ±10% under oblique light one meter away. Together, facility staff no longer receive "faucet won't flow / faucet flows by itself" complaints, nor need to rearrange decor to avoid strong light.

## V. Installation Instructions

### 5.1 Before Installation

1. First open water to flush the pipeline, removing sand, stone, and rust to avoid clogging the solenoid valve.
2. Confirm water pressure is 0.05MPa ~ 0.8MPa; below 0.05MPa requires a booster pump.
3. Confirm power method: adapter wiring or 4 AA batteries (wiring-free).
4. Do not point the sensing window directly at sunlight and strong lamps; avoid obstructions larger than 1cm within 120cm in front.
5. Drill standard single hole per counter, inlet G1/2.

### 5.2 Precautions

- ⚠️ Always shut off water circuit and power before installation and maintenance.
- ⚠️ Each AC-powered unit must have a separate power switch and reliable grounding.
- ⚠️ Use high-performance alkaline batteries; do not mix old and new; for long-term disuse, cut power to prevent leakage.
- ⚠️ Do not plug/unplug sensor module terminals under power.
- ⚠️ Keep controller away from direct strong light and mirror reflection; for multiple units side by side pay attention to spacing to avoid mutual interference from mirror reflection.

### 5.3 Installation Steps

1. Install faucet body per hole, connect G1/2 inlet hose.
2. Open water for pressure test, confirm no leakage at 0.05MPa / 1.0MPa.
3. Connect power (battery box or adapter), fix control box.
4. After power-on, LED flashes once, solenoid valve opens/closes once (self-check) then enters standby.
5. Test water-out and close by placing hand in sensing zone.
6. Set water-out mode per scenario (long flow / instant sensing).
7. Adjust sensing distance.

### 5.4 Power-on Self-check

After power-on, LED flashes once → solenoid valve opens/closes instantly → enters standby. At low voltage the LED flashes quickly to remind. During learning/calibration, do not continuously block the sensing window. If battery is below alarm voltage, the LED flashes at a fixed frequency to prompt.

### 5.5 Battery Replacement

When voltage ≤4.6V, the LED flashes quickly per sensing and cuts off water-out to remind battery replacement. To replace: shut water → open battery compartment → replace 4 same-brand new alkaline batteries → reinstall and re-self-check. For long-term disuse, remove batteries.
